Early RAG systems perform a single retrieval step per query: embed the
question, fetch the top matching chunks, and generate once. This works
well for simple factual questions but struggles with questions that
require combining evidence from multiple, separately retrieved sources,
a pattern known as multi-hop reasoning. Multi-hop retrieval addresses this
by allowing the system to issue several retrieval queries in sequence,
using the output of one retrieval step to decide what to search for next.

Agentic RAG extends this further by giving the language model control
over the retrieval process itself, rather than following a fixed
retrieve-then-generate pipeline. An agentic system can decide whether
retrieval is even necessary for a given query, choose among multiple
knowledge sources, reformulate a query that returned poor results, and
call retrieval repeatedly until it judges the gathered evidence
sufficient to answer.

A related direction is self-correcting or self-reflective RAG, where the
system evaluates its own draft answer against the retrieved evidence
before returning it, and re-retrieves or revises the answer if it detects
an unsupported claim. Over the next several years, RAG systems are
expected to move from static, single-pass pipelines toward these more
autonomous, iterative architectures, narrowing the gap between "search
and summarize" and genuine multi-step research assistance.
