Retrieval-augmented generation reduces hallucination compared to pure
parametric generation, but it does not eliminate the sources of error;
it mostly relocates them into the retrieval stage. A retrieval failure,
where the vector search returns chunks that are irrelevant or misses the
one chunk that actually contains the answer, produces a generator that is
grounded in the wrong evidence, and the model may either give a wrong
answer confidently or fail to answer at all.

Context window limits are a second constraint: only a finite number of
retrieved chunks can be placed into the prompt, so ranking and filtering
quality directly determines whether the most relevant evidence survives
into the final prompt. Stale or conflicting documents in the corpus are a
third problem; if the knowledge base contains two versions of the same
policy document, the retriever may surface both, and the generator has no
principled way to know which one is current unless the documents are
explicitly versioned or dated.

Even with correct retrieval, hallucination can still occur if the
generator ignores the provided context and falls back on its own
parametric knowledge, especially when the retrieved passages are only
partially relevant. Latency is a practical challenge too: retrieval,
ranking, and generation are sequential steps, and each one adds delay, so
production RAG systems must be carefully optimized to stay responsive.
Finally, ranking errors, where a genuinely relevant chunk is retrieved but
scored too low to make the final context window, and embedding drift from
model upgrades, both silently degrade answer quality in ways that can be
hard to detect without dedicated evaluation.
